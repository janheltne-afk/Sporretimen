In the conversation about medical school, John Erik describes how much use he got out
of visual methods and the **memory palace** when learning large volumes of material.

## What is a memory palace?

A memory palace (also called the *method of loci*) is an old memory technique in
which you place the things you need to remember at particular spots in a room or
along a route you know well – your own flat, say, or the way to work. When you later
need the information, you “walk” mentally through those places again.

## Getting started

1. **Pick a place you know well** – your childhood home, the route to the shop, or a
   room you know in detail.
2. **Settle on a fixed order** through the place, so you always “walk” the same way.
3. **Place what you need to remember** at distinct points along the route (door,
   sofa, window).
4. **Make the images vivid and slightly absurd** – the stranger and more visual, the
   easier to recall.
5. **Walk the route** a few times to set it.

## When does it suit?

It works particularly well for remembering lists, sequences and factual material. As
John Erik points out: there is no single study technique that works for everyone –
try things out and find what suits the way you learn.
