**Mel Robbins** is a podcast host and author, and *The Let Them Theory* (2024)
became one of the fastest-selling self-help books in years. The whole book rests
on two words – and on two more that most people forget.

## Let them

The first half: **let them.** If someone excludes you, let them. If someone will
not answer the message, let them. If someone dislikes your choice, let them. If
someone decides to believe something untrue about you, let them.

The idea is that a large share of our daily unease goes into trying to manage
other people's behaviour, opinions and feelings – which we cannot manage. Saying
“let them” is putting that work down.

## Let me

The second half, and the important one: **let me.** Let them do as they like –
let *me* decide what I do now.

Without it the theory is just resignation. Let them leave the party without you,
**let me** find people I enjoy. Let them not get in touch, **let me** choose
whether to keep the friendship going or let it go. Robbins is clear that it is
the second sentence that makes this an action rather than a shrug.

## Where it helps most

- **Adult friendship.** Closeness comes from proximity and repetition, and as an
  adult both disappear automatically. Very few friendships die of conflict – they
  die because nobody takes the initiative. Let them not call; let me call, if it
  matters to me.
- **Comparison.** Rather than fighting envy, use it. What you react most
  strongly to in others points fairly precisely at what you want yourself.
- **People who do not want to change.** You cannot talk anyone into quitting
  drinking, changing jobs or becoming who they do not want to be. Let them – and
  let me decide what I then do about my own situation.

## What it should not be used for

The book addresses this, and it deserves underlining. “Let them” is a tool
against the need to control others – not a recipe for tolerating everything.
Abuse, harassment, unsafety, your own children needing boundaries: those are not
situations you release. Used wrongly, the phrase becomes an excuse for not doing
something you should.

## On the origin

The idea is not new, and it is worth being open about that. Separating what you
control from what you do not is the core of stoicism, and appears in Epictetus
nearly two thousand years ago. In therapy it recurs as work on boundaries.

The phrase “let them” itself spread through a poem by **Cassie Phillips** that
went viral in 2022, and when the book appeared in 2024 a public discussion
followed about credit. Robbins has said she encountered the expression through
her daughter, and that the thought belongs to no one person. That is a
reasonable description, but the discussion belongs in any account of the book.

## Worth knowing

The book is long for two sentences, and repeats itself. “Theory” is marketing
language – this is not research, it is a device. But the device is short enough
to remember at the moment you need it, which is more than can be said for a
great deal else.

*This is not advice about any specific relationship.*
