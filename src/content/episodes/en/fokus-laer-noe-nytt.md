Most people recognise it: you sit down to do one thing, and discover twenty minutes
later that you are doing something else entirely. The question is what causes it –
and whether it is really your fault.

In this Learn something new episode Jan Sindre goes through two books that answer
that quite differently.

**Stolen Focus** (2022) by the journalist **Johann Hari** looks outwards: at
technology, working life, sleep, food and the environment around us. His central
point is that our attention is not merely slipping – it is being taken from us by
forces that profit from it, and that the problem therefore cannot be solved by
self-discipline alone.

**Hyperfocus** (2018) by **Chris Bailey** looks inwards: at how attention actually
works, how much of the day runs on autopilot, and how you deliberately drop into
*hyperfocus* on a single task. Bailey is just as interested in the opposite –
*scatterfocus*, letting the mind wander – and in the fact that the brain needs both.

We look at what the two agree on, where they pull in different directions, and what
is actually usable in an ordinary working day.

*The episode is a walk through what the books claim, not the final word on your brain
or your health. Sources are given in the episode.*
