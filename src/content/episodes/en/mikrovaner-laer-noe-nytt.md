A Learn something new episode about **small habits** – the starting point is James
Clear's book *Atomic Habits*.

The main idea is that we overestimate what we can manage in a day, and underestimate
what small changes do over years. The point is not large, dramatic efforts, but
habits so small they barely call on willpower.

## The four laws

The whole book hangs on four questions, and each has a reverse. To build a habit you
go down the left-hand column. To break one, you invert each of them.

<figure class="fig fig--matrix">
  <p class="fig__title">The same four levers, two directions</p>
  <table>
    <thead><tr><th scope="col">The lever</th><th scope="col">Building a good habit</th><th scope="col">Breaking a bad one</th></tr></thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><th scope="row">The cue</th><td>Make it <b>obvious</b> – the running shoes are out</td><td>Make it invisible – the phone on another floor</td></tr>
      <tr><th scope="row">The craving</th><td>Make it <b>attractive</b> – tie it to something you like</td><td>Make it unattractive – look at what it actually costs you</td></tr>
      <tr><th scope="row">The effort</th><td>Make it <b>easy</b> – as few steps as possible between you and the act</td><td>Make it hard – put friction in the way</td></tr>
      <tr><th scope="row">The reward</th><td>Make it <b>satisfying</b> – something you feel straight away</td><td>Make it unsatisfying – let it cost something to skip</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
  <figcaption>Note that none of the rows is about wanting it enough. They are about arranging the situation so the choice is easy before you are standing in it – and that is the whole difference from relying on motivation.</figcaption>
</figure>

## The identity beneath the habit

Clear's argument is that most people start at the wrong end. They set a result, find
a method, and hope that in the end it will make them a different sort of person. He
turns it round:

<figure class="fig fig--rings">
  <p class="fig__title">Three layers – and which one you start with</p>
  <div class="fig__rings">
    <div class="fig__ring"><b>Outcomes</b><small>What you want to achieve. “I want to lose five kilos.” This is the layer most people start on.</small></div>
    <div class="fig__ring"><b>Process</b><small>What you do. The training programme, the routine, the system.</small></div>
    <div class="fig__ring" data-core><b>Identity</b><small>Who you believe you are. “I am someone who trains.” Habits that stick, stick here.</small></div>
  </div>
  <figcaption>The difference shows up the day something goes wrong. If the goal is five kilos, a missed session is a defeat. If the starting point is that you are someone who trains, it is only a day without training – and someone who trains goes tomorrow.</figcaption>
</figure>

## One per cent

The number has become the book's trademark, and it is worth seeing what it actually
says.

  <p class="fig__claim">Missing once is fine. Never miss twice.</p>
  <p class="fig__example"><b>Why the rule is shaped this way:</b> it is not the lost day that topples a habit – it is the day after, when “I have already broken the run” becomes the reason to skip again.
