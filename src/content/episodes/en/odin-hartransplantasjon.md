This episode comes from an older podcast, recorded some years ago. Methods,
prices and clinics change, so some of what is said may be out of date today.
They say as much themselves in the introduction, and it is worth bearing in
mind.

**Odin** had started thinning on top. He went to Istanbul, lay on a table for
eight hours, and came home with 4,300 new follicles and a bloodied scalp.

This is the whole trip, as he tells it.

## Where the idea came from

It came from TikTok. Odin had been following the account **Snusleppa**, who had
been through a hair transplant, and then did what few people do: he sent a
message on Instagram and asked outright whether the clinic was any good. The
answer was positive.

Then came a second tip, from someone at his university college, about a former
student who had done the same. He met him on a night out, felt his hair, and
concluded that it looked good.

Then he messaged the clinic and booked.

## What he did not check

This is where the conversation is unusually honest, and it is one of the reasons
it is worth hearing.

<figure class="fig fig--rule">
  <p class="fig__claim">He booked the procedure without looking into the alternatives – and does not defend it.</p>
  <p class="fig__example"><b>What he had not checked:</b> what it would have cost in Norway. Whether it should have been done in Norway. Whether he ought to have been on medication, ointments or creams beforehand. How the procedure even worked.<br /><b>What he had:</b> one positive message from one person on Instagram, and a look at one former student's hair on a night out.<br />Jan Sindre, who is thinning himself, had by contrast visited a clinic in Oslo and been given an assessment: he has "thin Nordic hair", a thick band around the sides and thinner on top, and was told a transplant would leave the top just as thin. Odin had never asked for that assessment.</p>
</figure>

He had made up his mind about three things beforehand. All three turned out

## The day at the clinic

The order, as he describes it. The third step is the one that hurts, and the
sixth is the one most people do not know about.

<figure class="fig fig--flow" data-dir="ned">
  <p class="fig__title">Seven steps, one day</p>
  <ol>
    <li><span class="fig__box"><b>The paperwork</b><small>A contract stating the result cannot be perfect and that the clinic will make an attempt. He did not read it properly, and asks rhetorically what the alternative was – not signing and flying home without hair.</small></span></li>
    <li><span class="fig__box"><b>Shaving and marking up</b><small>The head is shaved. The doctor draws where the hairline will sit, and he got a say in that.</small></span></li>
    <li><span class="fig__box"><b>The anaesthetic</b><small>Around 40–50 injections into the scalp. This is the part that hurts.</small></span></li>
    <li><span class="fig__box"><b>The channels</b><small>The small openings at the front and back where the new follicles will go.</small></span></li>
    <li><span class="fig__box"><b>The extraction</b><small>4,300 follicles taken from the band at the back and sides. Three people working at once.</small></span></li>
    <li><span class="fig__box"><b>The sorting</b><small>The follicles are laid out on a table and sorted by thickness and quality. The thickest go at the front, where hair shows most.</small></span></li>
    <li><span class="fig__box"><b>The implantation</b><small>Four people with tweezers put them back, one at a time.</small></span></li>
  </ol>
  <figcaption>The sorting step is the surprise. This is not simply moving hair from one place to another – somebody assesses each individual follicle and places the best ones where they will show. Odin describes the extraction as sounding like a small digger excavating his follicles.</figcaption>
</figure>

## The pain

This is the single most useful piece of information in the episode for anyone
considering the same.

<figure class="fig fig--rule">
  <p class="fig__claim">The painful part is not the operation. It is the first five minutes.</p>
  <p class="fig__example"><b>How he describes the anaesthetic:</b> it did not feel like being injected, but like somebody standing behind him with a fork. His eyes watered. On his own scale he put it at <b>eight out of ten</b> – and adds the caveat himself straight away: it is his scale, and anyone who has been through worse would put a different number on it.<br /><b>The rest:</b> not painful. The head is numb, and you only feel that somebody is working up there. Afterwards he was stiff, but not in pain.</p>
</figure>

## The aftercare

The clinic has its own follow-up team that keeps in touch over WhatsApp. He
sends in photographs, they answer questions as they come up.

> They have performed a procedure on my body. In a way that is going to change
> quite considerably. So it is good that they look after me afterwards.

He describes the clinic as sterile, clean and professional, and says the nurse
he spoke to had taken an ordinary nursing degree, worked five years in a
hospital, and then specialised at a hair clinic for two years.

## The reactions back home

This is the part he had dreaded most, and the one that amounted to least.

People who knew him asked outright. People who did not wondered whether he had
been in a fight – he was, after all, covered in blood. Somebody at a bar touched
his head without asking, which he was not pleased about, though he understands
the impulse: he had been looking forward to touching the new hair himself.

> It was actually surprisingly little.

His reflection afterwards is that people stare at anything unusual – and that
what he did turned out not to be so unusual after all.

## What the conversation is really about

Towards the end the conversation turns, and this is where the episode becomes
something other than a travel account.

<figure class="fig fig--rule">
  <p class="fig__claim">The arithmetic is 35,000 kroner. What he describes buying is not primarily hair.</p>
  <p class="fig__example"><b>In his own words:</b> he has been called endless things for thinning on top. He is the sort of person who laughs most things off – but in the end it stops being funny, because you go home and go to bed.<br /><b>And concretely:</b> he describes being taken for a lecturer at a student bar, in his twenties. Jan Sindre, who is at the start of the same thing, says he does not know what he will do – and that he has long said he would simply shave it all off.</p>
</figure>

That is the part that makes the episode worth hearing even if you will never set
foot on a plane to Istanbul.

*Neither Odin nor Jan Sindre is a professional in this field. Everything said is
personal experience from one person's procedure at one clinic, at one point in
time. If you are considering something similar, talk to a qualified health
professional first – and there are good options in Norway.*
