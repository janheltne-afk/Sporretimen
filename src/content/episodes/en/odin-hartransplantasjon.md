This episode comes from an older podcast, recorded some years ago. Methods,
prices and clinics change, so some of what is said may be out of date today.
They say as much themselves in the introduction, and it is worth bearing in
mind.

**Odin** had started thinning on top. He saw a well-known person with new hair
on TikTok, sent them a message on Instagram, got a second tip from a fellow
student, and booked an appointment in Istanbul.

That was the extent of the research. He is entirely open about it: he never
checked what it would have cost in Norway, or whether he should have been on any
medication beforehand.

The conversation covers the whole trip: the journey down, the day at the clinic,
the pain, the rules afterwards, the price, and the reactions when he came home
to a small university town with a bloodied scalp.

## What surprised him most

That he was not alone. He had expected to be the only Norwegian flying to Turkey
to fix his hair, and instead met someone from Sydney in the taxi, three
Americans in the hotel reception, and at the clinic two Norwegians, a Dane and a
Swede. The youngest was 21.

And that nobody reacted when he got home. He had braced himself for stares and
comments, and got almost neither.

## What the numbers say

The procedure lasted between six and eight hours. 4,300 follicles were taken
out, sorted on a table by thickness, and put back with tweezers by four people
working at once. It cost 2,400 euros including accommodation, transport and
medication – around 35,000 kroner all in once the flights are counted.

On a pain scale he put the operation at eight out of ten, while stressing that
this is his own scale, and that the worst part was the anaesthetic rather than
the procedure.

## What the episode is really about

Towards the end the conversation turns. Odin has paid 35,000 kroner, and what he
describes buying is not primarily hair. He talks about the things people call
you when you are thinning, about being the sort of person who laughs it off –
and about how that stops working at the end of the day, when you go home and go
to bed.

That is the part that makes the episode worth hearing even if you will never set
foot on a plane to Istanbul.

*Neither Odin nor Jan Sindre is a professional in this field. Everything said is
personal experience from one person's procedure at one clinic, at one point in
time. If you are considering something similar, talk to a qualified health
professional first – and there are good options in Norway.*
